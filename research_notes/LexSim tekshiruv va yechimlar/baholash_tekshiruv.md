## Q1. Which weight table fits an AI-judged team moot on a commercial case?

### Takeaway
Use the **proposed table (legal 30, rebuttal/answers 25, facts 20, participation/organisation 15, manners/delivery 10)**. It is Jessup 2025's published 30/25/20/15/10 profile almost exactly, and the other real scoresheets match its order: law ≥ questions ≥ facts > delivery. The frontend's original table puts facts 30 above legal 15, and no scoresheet found does that. A strict SQE2 50/50 split is the wrong instrument for a competitive moot. Keep it for LexSim's individual competency tasks.

### Cited Findings
- Jessup 2025 per oralist: law 30, Q&A 25, facts 20, style/poise 15, organisation/time 10, total 50–100. — [ILSA 2025 scoresheet](http://web.archive.org/web/20250218030633/https://www.ilsa.org/Jessup/Jessup2025/Admin/2025JessupOralRoundsScoresheet%20Final%20Fillable.pdf)
- The Vis is the leading *commercial* (CISG/arbitration) moot. It scores holistically on 50–100 with four unweighted criteria: (1) Organization and Preparation, which includes "If rebuttal is used, is it used effectively?"; (2) Knowledge of the facts and the law; (3) Presentation; (4) Handling Questions. Arbitrators must not judge the merits, and must "attempt to avoid the 'halo effect'". "Mistakes or difficulty in use of the English language should not be penalized". — [33rd Vis instructions](https://www.vismoot.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/33rd-Vis-Moot_General-Arbitrator-Instructions_FINAL.pdf)
- WHRMC 2025: facts/analysis 25, law 25, organisation/clarity/questions 25, ingenuity/persuasiveness 25. — [WHRMC 2025](https://www.chr.up.ac.za/images/moots/world_moot/2025/EN_Appendix_D_Scoring_sheet.pdf)
- Price 2026–27: 9 unweighted criteria. About 5 of them are legal substance (legal analysis applied to facts, authorities, problem recognition, submissions, original thought), 2 are delivery/communication, 1 is logic/clarity, and 1 is rebuttal. — [Price Rules 2026–27](https://www.law.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2026-11/2026-2027%20Competition%20Rules%20Price%20Media%20Law%20Moot%20Court.pdf)
- Berkeley McBaine 2025: opening 15, argument 35, questions 35, conclusion/rebuttal 5, delivery 10 (read in an earlier session). — [McBaine 2025](https://law.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/2025-McBaine-Oral-Argument-Scoring-Sheet.pdf)
- SQE2 advocacy: skills (language/behaviour, structure, persuasiveness, engaging audience, key facts) and law (correct, comprehensive, and ethics from 2026) weighted 50/50 overall. — [SRA 2026 spec](https://sqe.sra.org.uk/docs/default-source/pdfs/sqe2-assesment-specification-(from-1-september-2026).pdf?sfvrsn=6918c847_2)

### Inferences
- **Reject the frontend original.** Every weighted sheet (Jessup, WHRMC, McBaine) gives law at least as much as facts. Jessup gives law 1.5× facts, and Price lists more legal criteria than any other kind. Weighting legal at 15, the lowest, rewards retelling the story over argument. In a commercial case that means reciting the contract instead of applying CISG or the Civil Code.
- **Prefer the proposed table.** Its order (legal > rebuttal > facts > participation > manners) matches Jessup point for point. It also matches McBaine's heavy weight on questions and Vis/Price's explicit rebuttal criterion. The only difference from Jessup is that the last two buckets are swapped: participation/organisation 15 vs 10, and manners 10 vs 15. That swap is justified for an *AI* judge working from an STT transcript:
  - delivery, poise and eye contact cannot be observed reliably from text;
  - Vis forbids penalising language errors;
  - Uzbek ASR WER is about 14–26% (earlier notes).

  Organisation (roadmap, time use, fair split of speaking between teammates) can be checked deterministically from timestamps.
- **Why not the SQE2 50/50 split.** SQE2 is a pass/fail competency exam for one candidate across many stations. It is not a ranking of teams in adversarial argument. It is still a sanity check: in the proposed table, legal + the legal part of rebuttal is about 30 + ~12 ≈ 42–45%, and skills are about 55%. That is close to 50/50, so the table is consistent with SQE2 without copying it. Keep SQE2 50/50 for LexSim's individual tasks: consultation, attendance note, document drafting.
- **Ethics.** No moot scoresheet has an ethics bucket. Keep ethics as the cap/gate already in the report: a confirmed misconduct caps the score and routes it to a mentor. This mirrors SQE2, where ethics is marked only when the task contains an ethics point.
- **Keep these AI-judge instructions, all citable from Vis 33rd:**
  - score each team member individually;
  - do not score the merits of the side argued;
  - avoid the halo effect;
  - do not penalise language mistakes.
- **Scale.** Show 50–100 with the 33rd Vis bands (now internally consistent), or Jessup's Weak/Average/Strong bands per category. Jessup's per-category bands translate directly: legal 15–19/20–25/26–30, rebuttal 12–15/16–21/22–25, facts 10–12/13–17/18–20. For the proposed participation 15 and manners 10 buckets, reuse Jessup's 15- and 10-point bands (8–10/11–13/14–15 and 5–6/7–8/9–10).
- **Fix the report text in four places:**
  - Jessup style = 15 and organisation = 10 (no longer "not found");
  - SQE2 ethics = separate third law criterion, marked only where assessed (no longer [TT]);
  - LexRubric κ 0.589 is judge vs expert;
  - "formative = out of scope" needs the 3(b) "steer the learning process" caveat. LexSim stays outside 3(b) only while students use it voluntarily and its scores do not feed grades or assessment.
