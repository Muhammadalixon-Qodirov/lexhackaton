# Kazakhstan and CIS B2B commercial disputes for an Uzbek economic court training simulator

Scope note: I verified 3 AIFC Court judgments by downloading and reading the full PDF text. I could identify Kazakhstan Supreme Court (ВС РК) cassation rulings only from search-result snippets and press reports. The aggregator that hosted their full texts (pavlodar.com/zakon/sot/...) now returns 404 for every ruling I tried, so their reasoning is **unverified beyond the snippet level**. Treat those entries as leads, not as finished scenarios. Uzbek Civil Code (ГК РУз) article equivalents are given from general knowledge and are marked "verify". I did not check them against lex.uz in this session.

## Q1: Which Kazakhstan Supreme Court normative resolutions and case reviews include concrete fact patterns on these topics?

### Takeaway
I did not locate a specific ВС РК normative resolution on penalty reduction (Art. 297 ГК РК) or on force majeure / COVID-19. I did find several concrete ВС РК cassation rulings from 2022 to 2025 on penalty reduction, insurance-payout refusals and force majeure in supply. Their full texts could not be retrieved, so only headline facts are available. The strongest documented one is the 2024 ammunition-supplier force-majeure case, where the Supreme Court reversed both lower courts.

### Cited Findings

**Lead case A: force majeure from an EU transit ban, supplier v. Ministry of Internal Affairs (MVD) (B2G supply contract, contentious, reversed on cassation)**
- Facts: an unnamed ТОО (LLP) contracted to supply 4,500 thermobaric rounds from Bulgaria to the MVD for training ranges. The contract was worth over 800 million tenge, the delivery deadline was 15 November, and the contract ran to 31 December (the year is inferred as 2023 from a 14 Nov 2024 article). The supplier bought the goods, prepared them for shipment and paid for air transport. On 22 December the EU banned transit of this cargo type across EU territory, and the Bulgarian factory was closed until 2 January. — [Informburo](https://informburo.kz/novosti/verxovnyi-sud-vstupilsya-za-postavshhika-boepripasov-dlya-mvd-rk-kotoryi-narusil-sroki-iz-za-ogranicenii-evrosoyuza)
- Claim: the supplier asked the court to order the MVD to remove obstacles to obtaining 2024 permits. These were an import licence and an MFA overflight permit for the route Bulgaria–Turkey–Azerbaijan–Georgia–Kazakhstan. Separately, the Almaty economic court refused the MVD's request to declare the supplier an "unfair procurement participant". — [Informburo](https://informburo.kz/novosti/verxovnyi-sud-vstupilsya-za-postavshhika-boepripasov-dlya-mvd-rk-kotoryi-narusil-sroki-iz-za-ogranicenii-evrosoyuza)
- Lower courts: the Astana economic court dismissed the claim and the appellate court upheld that. Their reasoning was that the goods were not delivered, the contract term had expired, and the force majeure argument was untenable. — [Informburo](https://informburo.kz/novosti/verxovnyi-sud-vstupilsya-za-postavshhika-boepripasov-dlya-mvd-rk-kotoryi-narusil-sroki-iz-za-ogranicenii-evrosoyuza)
- Supreme Court: it granted the claim. It held that the supplier took all measures within its control. It found the delay was caused by force majeure, because geopolitical events and military action forced a route change that required new permits. It also noted the customer was notified in time. — [Informburo](https://informburo.kz/novosti/verxovnyi-sud-vstupilsya-za-postavshhika-boepripasov-dlya-mvd-rk-kotoryi-narusil-sroki-iz-za-ogranicenii-evrosoyuza); also reported by [Azattyq Rýhy](https://rus.azattyq-ruhy.kz/news/76481-kompanii-prishlos-suditsia-s-mvd-kazakhstana-iz-za-fors-mazhora-v-evrope/amp)
- Missing details: the case number, the articles cited and the parties' names are not in the press reports. The norm is presumably Art. 359(2) ГК РК (force majeure as a ground for release from liability), but this is my inference.
- Caveat: the customer is a state body, so this is B2G rather than strictly B2B. The issues are still purely contractual.

**Lead case B: insurance payout refusal for burnt cargo (B2B: LLP v. JSC insurer)**
- ВС РК ruling 29.01.2024, case No. 6001-23-00-3Г/6188. Per the search snippet, the cargo burned and the cargo owner (a ТОО) claimed about 57.6 million tenge. The ruling issued a new decision granting the claim: it declared unlawful the refusal of АО «Страховая компания «Amanat» to pay and ordered payment of 25,000,000 tenge. — [pavlodar.com (now 404)](https://pavlodar.com/zakon/sot/fb246dbc8ebbf837315279af5f90d46f)
- Related ruling 13.03.2024, case No. 6001-24-00-3ГП/97, apparently the same dispute at a later stage. Per the snippet, the first instance refused payment because the shipper had breached the Rules for carriage of goods by road. The appellate court then held the insurer's refusal unlawful and awarded 25,000,000 tenge, and the АО sought cassation for misapplication of substantive law. — [pavlodar.com (now 404)](https://pavlodar.com/zakon/sot/3c9f0a1e5d77a3751f7037ef02ad2125)

**Penalty reduction under Art. 297 ГК РК (snippets)**
- ВС РК ruling 30.07.2024, case No. 6001-24-00-3ГП/324: a penalty was reduced under Art. 297 from 132,877,640 tenge to 13,287,764 tenge, i.e., about tenfold. — [pavlodar.com (now 404)](https://pavlodar.com/zakon/sot/50e854e75db3ec665d57eb169300e740)
- ВС РК ruling 01.07.2024, case No. 6001-24-00-3Г/3125, quotes the rule that "if the penalty payable is excessively large compared with the creditor's losses, the court may reduce it at the debtor's request". — [pavlodar.com (now 404)](https://pavlodar.com/zakon/sot/b3397cd05891f9bb053014c2cae2c624)
- ВС РК ruling 15.01.2024, case No. 6001-23-00-3Г/6503: per the snippet, the Supreme Court said the lower courts reduced a penalty without proper grounds, relying only on the defendant's disagreement with its size and a bare reference to Art. 297. This is a useful "common mistake" point: mere disagreement is not enough, and the debtor must prove the penalty is excessive compared with the creditor's losses. — [pavlodar.com (now 404)](https://pavlodar.com/zakon/sot/ca28758c27de8abc90edae48301a5162)
- Other snippets mention reduction to 10% of the accrued amount, and reduction to 70,000,000 tenge in a supply case (case numbers not confirmed). — same search results, pavlodar.com

**Force majeure in supply (snippets)**
- In ВС РК supply cases, courts checked whether the performance deadline fell within the state-of-emergency period. In one case the delivery deadline was outside that period, so the force majeure defence was refused. — [pavlodar.com search results](https://pavlodar.com/zakon/sot/3136302443aea05bb894b1a582f677a2)
- In another case, a delay in a US export licence was held not to be force majeure, with reference to Art. 359(2) ГК РК. Courts also gave little weight to a force majeure certificate that did not show circumstances linked to performance of the specific contract. Higher market prices without a certificate were also rejected. — search snippets of ВС РК rulings (case numbers not verified), e.g. [6001-24-00-ЗГ/5236](https://pavlodar.com/zakon/sot/3136302443aea05bb894b1a582f677a2)
- ВС РК ruling 16.07.2024, case No. 6001-24-00-3ГП/285: a supplier sought recognition of force majeure for the whole delay period, plus the price of goods, the contractual penalty and indexation. The Almaty specialized interdistrict economic court (СМЭС) dismissed it and the appellate court partly modified the decision. The Supreme Court outcome is unknown. — [pavlodar.com (now 404)](https://pavlodar.com/zakon/sot/9a2b14be2d8e3deb2a43454a38af333a)

### Inferences
- Lead case A is the best Kazakh "force majeure / border closure" scenario. The Supreme Court reversed both lower courts, so the case is debatable by construction. A student for the supplier must prove three things: (1) an extraordinary and unavoidable event (Art. 359(2) ГК РК; Uzbek analogue Art. 333 ГК РУз, verify); (2) causation between the event and the delay; (3) timely notice to the counterparty and mitigation steps such as buying the goods and arranging alternative routes. The customer's counter-arguments are that the contract term has expired, that the supplier carries commercial risk, and that the ban came after the 15 November deadline. That last timing point is a strong one the press report does not address, and it is where the scenario becomes genuinely debatable.
- Penalty reduction under Art. 297 ГК РК corresponds to Art. 326 ГК РУз (verify). The teaching point from case 6001-23-00-3Г/6503 is that the debtor must plead and prove excessiveness compared with the creditor's losses. A creditor arguing against reduction should stress the contractual freedom of B2B parties and the absence of evidence of disproportion.
- The burnt-cargo insurance case (Lead case B) turns on whether the shipper's breach of carriage rules justifies the insurer's refusal. Insurance-refusal norms in ГК РК are in Chapter 40 (e.g., Art. 841, grounds for exemption from payout — verify article). The Uzbek analogue is in ГК РУз Chapter 52 (verify).

## Q2: Which AIFC Court judgments (English, court.aifc.kz) involve B2B contract disputes with detailed facts and reasoning?

### Takeaway
The AIFC Court publishes full English judgments that apply the Kazakh Civil Code. The best training case is **Samruk Service Group LLP v Tethys Aktau II LLP (AIFC-C/SCC/2025/0063, 10 Aug 2026)**, a fully contested construction acceptance and defects case. Two shorter supply / payment cases are also verified: Kostanai Minerals and Shanghai Construction v TeksheTas.

### Cited Findings

**Case 1 (top pick): Samruk Service Group LLP v Tethys Aktau II LLP, AIFC Small Claims Court, Case No. AIFC-C/SCC/2025/0063, Judgment 10 August 2026, Justice Josephine Higgs KC** — [Judgment PDF](https://court.aifc.kz/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/AIFC-Court-Case-No.-63-of-2025-Judgment_ENG.pdf); [case page](https://court.aifc.kz/?p=16564)
- Contract: Construction Contract dated 2 May 2025 for the "Beach Bar and Houses" project at the Tetysblu theme park, Aktau. The judgment numbers it both TA-0237 (para 1.1) and TA-2037 (para 3.1), an inconsistency in the source.
  - Price: KZT 18,000,000 in two tranches. The first tranche of KZT 10,000,000 was paid on 6 May 2025; the final KZT 8,000,000 was payable on acceptance under cl. 3.2.
  - Timing: 40-day installation period, starting within 3 days of receipt of the first tranche.
  - Scope: defined by Appendix 1, which says "Detailed instructions regarding the technical task are provided by the Engineer from the Customer's side", and Appendix 2 (specification table with images and dimensions).
  - Other clauses: cl. 8.1 requires amendments to be in writing and signed; cl. 5.2 sets Kazakh law and AIFC Court jurisdiction.
- Applicable law: the parties agreed that ГК РК Arts. 616–639 (general contract-work / подряд rules) and 651–666 (construction contracts) apply. The court relied on Art. 654 (duty to build per the design documentation), Art. 635(1) (proportionate price reduction for defects) and Art. 630(6) (expert examination on acceptance disputes, which the claimant invoked).
- Chronology:
  - 13 Jun 2025: defendant's inspection with photographs.
  - 23 Jun 2025: defendant's "Claim No. 1" listing defects and delay.
  - 24 Jun 2025: claimant's pre-trial claim demanding signature of the completion certificate and KZT 8m.
  - 15 Jul 2025: joint inspection, followed the same day by "Claim No. 2", a reasoned refusal of acceptance.
  - 7 Nov 2025: claim filed.
  - 21 Nov 2025: defence, with an inspection act and a defects table.
  - 27 Feb 2026: counterclaim. The defendant switched its remedy from rectification to proportionate price reduction under Art. 635(1).
  - 29 May 2026: expert application refused.
  - 10 Jun 2026: hearing with 6 witnesses.
- Claimant's evidence: extensive photos, videos and WhatsApp videos, and audio recordings. It said the recordings showed that the Chief Engineer, Rinat Abdrakhmanov, accepted the works. Its witness statements were served late, after the 27 May deadline.
- Claimant's arguments:
  1. Works were completed per the specification.
  2. The customer's representatives supervised and approved details on site.
  3. The defendant failed to order an expert examination under Art. 630(6), which shows acceptance.
  4. Acceptance by use: the defendant held events and festivals at the bar.
  5. The defendant interrupted the works four times.
- Defendant's evidence: inspection acts of 13 Jun and 21 Nov 2025, a defects table, two rectification estimates (KZT 7.2m and KZT 6.5m), and witness statements from its accountant-auditor and managing director.
- Defendant's arguments: defects and deviations (exposed metal frames, poor wood, chipboard instead of "1st grade pine", a missing artificial-stone countertop, poor finishing), plus delay. It said use of the bar was mitigation of loss, not acceptance, and that the engineer had no authority to amend the contract (cl. 8.1).
- Holdings:
  1. The works were *substantially* completed, but some items did not conform.
  2. Details approved by the customer's engineer cannot be complained of. Those approvals were "detailed instructions" within Appendix 1, not contract amendments, so cl. 8.1 was not breached.
  3. No acceptance occurred. Refusing to sign the certificate was justified, because reasoned refusals were sent on 23 Jun and 15 Jul.
  4. Failure to order an expert examination is not acceptance.
  5. Commercial use was reasonable mitigation, not acceptance.
  6. The defendant was entitled to a price reduction under Art. 635(1). Its quotes were excessive compared with the KZT 18m price and were prepared after use-related wear. With no expert evidence, the court assessed the reduction at KZT 3,500,000.
  7. The claimant's damage-from-interruptions point failed for lack of evidence and of a formal claim.
- Expert evidence: the application was refused because the works were not highly technical, the record was extensive, and an expert would be disproportionate to the amount at stake.
- Order: the defendant pays KZT 4,500,000 plus the KZT 132,000 court fee, total KZT 4,632,000, within 28 days. The claimant's KZT 800,000 legal costs were not recoverable under SCC Rule 26.9, and the defendant's KZT 1,680,000 costs application was dismissed. The claimant's procedural lapses were not "unreasonable behaviour".
- Representation: the claimant was represented by its director; the defendant by Kirill Sharapov of ALC Attorneys LLP.

**Case 2: Kostanai Minerals JSC (Kazakhstan) v Factory Dnepropetrovskaya Volna OJSC (Ukraine), AIFC SCC, Case No. AIFC-C/SCC/2021/014, Judgment 1 March 2022, Justice Charles Banner QC** — [Judgment PDF](https://court.aifc.kz/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/AIFC-Court-Case-No.14-of-2021_Judgment-ENG.pdf); [case page](https://court.aifc.kz/judgments/case-no-14-of-2021/)
- Contract: Contract 04/01 of 4 Jan 2018 for the supply of chemical goods, priced at USD 573,750. An additional agreement of 12 Feb 2018 raised the price to USD 1,644,750. Cl. 3.1 allowed delivery in parts, cl. 5.2 required an invoice per batch, and cl. 5.3 set payment within 90 days of shipment.
- Deliveries and payments:
  - Goods worth USD 541,875 were delivered between 26 Feb 2018 and 19 Apr 2019. Several variations deferred payment.
  - The buyer paid USD 100,000 on 20 May 2019.
  - On 1 Jun 2020 the Economic Court of Zaporozhye region (Ukraine) ordered payment of USD 308,000, which was paid.
- Mediation and claim: AIFC IAC mediation produced a Mediation Agreement of 15 Nov 2021 to pay the remaining USD 133,875 by 22 Nov 2021, with AIFC Court jurisdiction and Kazakh law. The buyer did not pay. The claim was filed 27 Dec 2021, with no interest claimed.
- Procedure: the court extended the defence deadline to 15 Feb 2022 because of the January 2022 unrest. The defendant filed nothing.
- Holding: claim allowed under ГК РК Arts. 272, 273, 438 and/or 469; USD 133,875 payable within 7 days. The court warned that this behaviour could justify costs under Rule 26.9(2) in future.

**Case 3: Shanghai Construction Group Co. Ltd Kazakhstan Branch v TeksheTas Company LLP, AIFC SCC, Case No. AIFC-C/SCC/2022/0024, Judgment 24 March 2023, Justice Charles Banner KC** — [Judgment PDF](https://court.aifc.kz/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Judgment-AIFC-Court-Case-No.-24-of-2022-ENG.pdf)
- Facts: supply contract of 21 Feb 2020 for crushed stone. The buyer claimed KZT 16,839,426, the difference between prepayments made to the end of 2021 and the stone actually delivered (unjust enrichment / return of unworked prepayment pattern).
- Procedure: filed 29 Nov 2022 in the Specialised Interdistrict Economic Court of Astana. On 7 Dec 2022 the parties signed a Transfer of Cases Consent Form, and on 23 Dec 2022 Chief Justice Lord Mance accepted jurisdiction.
- Outcome: the defendant admitted the claim (15 Feb 2023) but asked for payment deferral to 15 Apr 2023, citing a difficult financial situation and seasonality. The court allowed the claim and granted the deferral.

**Case 4 (thin): Documentolog Global Limited v Kostanay Metal Structures Plant LLP, AIFC-C/SCC/2022/0025, 5 Apr 2023, Lord Banner KC**
- Unpaid IT services. A KZT 350,000 payment was made after filing and deducted; the defendant was ordered to pay the KZT 564,969.65 balance. Too thin for a full scenario. — [case page](https://court.aifc.kz/judgments/case-no-25-of-2022/)

### Inferences
- Samruk v Tethys is an ideal construction acceptance simulator case. Each side wins some points, the amount is small, and the key questions are genuinely contestable:
  - Is the customer's commercial use acceptance by conduct, or mitigation?
  - Do an on-site engineer's approvals bind the customer despite a written-amendments clause?
  - Does failure to request an expert examination amount to acceptance?
  - How should a court quantify a price reduction without an expert?
- Uzbek analogues (verify on lex.uz): contract work (подряд) and construction contracts are in ГК РУз Chapter 37. Kazakh Art. 635 (proportionate price reduction) has a quality-of-work counterpart in the Uzbek general contract-work articles.
- Common student mistakes this case exposes:
  - the contractor relying on an unsigned completion certificate and on oral or audio "acceptance";
  - serving witness statements late, which costs weight;
  - submitting untranslated audio;
  - the customer inflating rectification quotes, and switching remedies late (rectification to price reduction);
  - the customer failing to document defects jointly at the time.
- TeksheTas is a template for "return of an unworked prepayment" in supply. Uzbek law offers both a contractual route (supply of goods) and an unjust-enrichment route (ГК РУз Art. 1023, verify). The real case was uncontested, though, so the scenario needs invented counter-arguments. Label them clearly as hypothetical.
