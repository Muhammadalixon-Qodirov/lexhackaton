Scope note: this builds on `research_notes/Sud jarayonidagi psixologik taktikalar/ethics_training.md` (Hampel/NITA critique method, ethics rules) and `reports/LexSim manbalar asosi.md` (3637 exam format, ethics-rule bands). ## Q1. UK SQE2: skills and law criteria, A–F scale, weighting, pass mark

### Takeaway
SQE2 is the most directly adoptable model. Each station has a short list of named criteria, split into "skills" and "application of law (including ethics)". Each criterion is graded A–F, converted to 5–0 marks, and skills and law are weighted equally overall. Pass/fail is decided by one overall standard-set pass mark, not per station. The six-level descriptor ladder ("superior / clearly satisfactory / marginal pass / marginal fail / clearly unsatisfactory / poor") is citable verbatim and maps cleanly onto LexSim's 0–5 per-criterion scale.

### Cited Findings
**Current specification (applies from 1 September 2026; PDF created 30 April 2026)** — [SRA, SQE2 Assessment Specification and FLK (from 1 Sept 2026), PDF](https://sqe.sra.org.uk/docs/default-source/pdfs/sqe2-assesment-specification-(from-1-september-2026).pdf?sfvrsn=6918c847_2)
- Grading scale (verbatim): "A. Superior performance: well above the competency requirements of the assessment; B. Clearly satisfactory: clearly meets the competency requirements…; C. Marginal pass: on balance, just meets…; D. Marginal fail: on balance, just fails to meet…; E. Clearly unsatisfactory: clearly does not meet…; F. Poor performance: well below the competency requirements of the assessment."
- Graders are "trained assessors making global professional judgments related to the standard of competency".
- Conversion: "converted into numerical marks such that A = 5 marks and F = 0 marks."
- Weighting: "The marking criteria for each of the stations has been divided into marks for skills and marks for application of law (including Ethics and professional conduct). In arriving at a final mark for the candidate across all assessments, skills and application of law are weighted equally. This is to make sure that adequate weighting is given to the quality of the advice provided."
- Interview: "The interviewing station will be marked by the assessor playing the role of the client and will be marked on skills only." The attendance note and all other stations are marked by a solicitor on both skills and law.
- Application of law has three sub-criteria from 2026: "legally correct", "legally comprehensive", and "ethics and professional conduct". Correct means identifying the correct principles and applying them to the facts. Comprehensive means "the extent to which candidates have identified and applied the correct legal principles, as well as … a comprehensive analysis of the legal issues."
- Ethics: "candidates will be expected to identify the ethical and professional conduct issue(s) and exercise judgment to resolve them honestly and with integrity."
- Standard: the "newly qualified (Day One) solicitor". Candidates "will not be expected to know or address detail that a newly qualified solicitor would look up". Outside the Legal Research station, they "are not required to recall specific case names, or cite statutory or regulatory authorities".
- A non-exhaustive list of what "application of law" includes: "Identifying relevant legal principles; Applying legal principles to factual issues…; Assessing information to identify key issues and risks; Recognising inconsistencies and gaps in information; Evaluating the quality and reliability of information; … Reaching reasoned decisions supported by relevant evidence."
- The spec also tells assessors not to deduct marks for some spelling, grammar and formatting errors. The exact conditions were not extracted.

**Per-station criteria (verbatim from the SRA pilot specification; the 2026 spec keeps the same six station types)** — [SRA, SQE2 Pilot Assessment Specification](https://www.sra.org.uk/sra/policy/solicitors-qualifying-examination/pilot/sqe2-pilot-assessment-specification/)
- **Client interview (skills only):** "Establish and maintain an effective relationship with the client so as to build trust and confidence"; "Communicate and explain in a way that is suitable for the client to understand"; "Listen to the client and use questioning effectively to enable the client to tell the solicitor what is important to them"; "Conduct themselves in a professional manner and treat the client with courtesy, respect and politeness"; "Demonstrate client-focus in their approach to the client and the issues".
- **Attendance note / legal analysis.** Skills: "Obtain all relevant information"; "Identify appropriate next steps"; "Provide client-focussed advice". Law: "correct analysis…", "comprehensive analysis of the application of the law to the client's situation", plus the ethics criterion.
- **Advocacy / persuasive oral presentation.** Skills: "Use language and behaviour appropriate to the context"; "Use a clear and logical structure for the presentation"; "Make a persuasive argument"; "Engage the audience appropriately"; "Include all key relevant facts". Law: "Apply the law correctly…", "Apply the law comprehensively to the client's situation", plus ethics.
- **Case and matter analysis.** Skills: "Identify relevant facts"; "Suggest and advise on client focussed options, strategies or solutions"; "Use clear, precise, concise and acceptable language". Law: correct, comprehensive, ethics.
- **Legal research.** Skills: "Identify and use relevant sources and information"; "Suggest advice which is client focussed and addresses the client's problem"; clear language. Law: correct, comprehensive, ethics.
- **Legal writing.** Skills: "Include relevant facts"; "Use a logical structure"; "Content is recipient focussed"; language "appropriate to the recipient". Law: "Apply and/or explain the law correctly/comprehensively", plus ethics.
- **Legal drafting.** Skills: "Use clear, precise, concise and acceptable language"; "Structure the document appropriately and logically". Law: "Draft a document which is legally correct (ie accurately gives effect to client instructions)"; "legally comprehensive (ie accurately gives effect to all the client's instructions)", plus ethics.

**Pass mark**
- There is one pass mark for SQE2 as a whole, calculated separately for each cohort, and reports show it on a scaled score (300). Station percentages for skills and law are calculated and averaged. Retrieved via search snippets of the SRA results pages, so the wording is not verbatim. — [SRA, understanding SQE2 results](https://sqe.sra.org.uk/sqe-results/results-resits/understanding-sqe2-results); [SRA Marking and Standard Setting Policy](https://sqe.sra.org.uk/policies/marking-and-standard-setting-policy)
- Standard setting uses the borderline regression method, with examiners also giving a global pass / marginal pass / marginal fail / fail grade. **[UNVERIFIED — third-party sources only]** — [sqepractice.co.uk](https://sqepractice.co.uk/sqe2-marking-criteria); [fqps.co.uk](https://fqps.co.uk/blog/post/sqe2-changes-2026-2027-guide)
- From September 2026, ethics reportedly gets its own 0–5 mark within "law". **[UNVERIFIED — third-party summary]**. The official spec confirms only that law now explicitly "includ[es] Ethics and professional conduct". — [SRA post-Sept-2026 info page](https://sqe.sra.org.uk/sqe-results/results-resits/understanding-sqe2-results/post-september-2026-information)
- The SRA publishes "Performance Indicators" showing competent and non-competent performance for each criterion. These were not retrieved because the SRA site is a JS app the fetcher could not render. — [SRA SQE2 specification page](https://sqe.sra.org.uk/assessments/sqe2-assessments/sqe2-specification)

### Inferences
- **Adopt the A–F ladder as LexSim's 0–5 per-criterion scale.** Use the SRA wording translated into Uzbek, keep the 6 levels, and set "C = marginal pass" as the competency line. This answers "why 3/5?" with a citable descriptor.
- **Mapping SQE2 to LexSim's six criteria:**
  - legal = "legally correct" + "legally comprehensive"
  - ethics = SQE2 ethics criterion
  - facts = "Include all key relevant facts" / "Identify relevant facts"
  - communication = "language and behaviour appropriate to the context" / "clear and logical structure" / "persuasive argument" / "engage the audience"
  - document = drafting criteria (legally effective; gives effect to all instructions; structure and language)
  - procedure has no direct SQE2 equivalent, because SQE2 does not test procedural steps in isolation. Treat it as a LexSim-specific sub-criterion of "legally correct", anchored in IPK/FPK articles.
- **Weighting rule.** Make skills (facts + communication + document form) and law (legal + procedure + ethics) 50/50, citing SQE2 equal weighting. This would replace the current unsourced 30/15/15/15/15/10 weights.
- **Consultation module.** Score the client-interaction part on skills only, with the AI "client" acting as the assessor, exactly like SQE2. Score the follow-up note on skills + law.

- Verbatim 2026 per-station criteria and the Performance Indicators could not be extracted. The SRA HTML pages are JS-rendered, and the 2026 PDF covers the general marking section only.
- The exact borderline-regression procedure was not read in the primary policy.

## Q2. US: NCBE MPT, trial-advocacy evaluation, clinical/competency frameworks

### Takeaway
The MPT gives a citable six-skill taxonomy, derived from the ABA MacCrate Report, that maps almost one-to-one to LexSim's criteria. Its grading is relative (rank-ordering on 1–6), which is a useful idea for moot leaderboards but not for absolute feedback. IAALS gives the empirical case for scoring character and professionalism, such as integrity, alongside legal skill.

### Cited Findings
- The MPT "examines six fundamental lawyering skills", based on the MacCrate Report's "Statement of Fundamental Lawyering Skills" (ABA 1992). — [NCBE, MPT Skills Tested (PDF)](https://www.ncbex.org/sites/default/files/2023-01/MPT_Skills_Tested_2023.pdf)
  1. "Problem solving" (identify and diagnose the problem; generate alternative solutions; develop and implement a plan of action)
  2. "Legal analysis and reasoning" (identify and formulate legal issues; identify relevant legal rules within given materials; formulate, elaborate and evaluate legal theories; "criticize and synthesize legal arguments")
  3. "Factual analysis" (identify relevant facts; determine the need for factual investigation; "evaluate the information that has been gathered")
  4. "Communication" ("assess the perspective of the recipient"; "organize and express ideas with precision, clarity, logic, and economy")
  5. "Organization and management of a legal task" ("allocate time, effort, and resources efficiently"; "perform and complete tasks within time constraints")
  6. "Recognizing and resolving ethical dilemmas" (knowledge of the sources of ethical standards and how they are enforced; "ability to recognize and resolve ethical dilemmas")
- MPT grading is relative. NCBE trains graders on a 1–6 scale, where 6 is for the best papers in the grader's pool, and urges graders to use the full range rather than compressing scores to 3–4. Jurisdictions may use other scales. Retrieved via search snippets of NCBE articles. — [The Bar Examiner, "It's All Relative: MEE and MPT Grading"](https://thebarexaminer.ncbex.org/article/june-2016/its-all-relative-mee-and-mpt-grading-that-is-2/); [13 best practices for grading essays and performance tests](https://thebarexaminer.ncbex.org/article/mee-and-mpt/13-best-practices-for-grading-essays-and-performance-tests)
- Example of local weighting: Texas weights the MPT at 20% of the UBE total and scores each task on a 0–6 scale (search snippet). — [Texas Board of Law Examiners](https://ble.texas.gov/scoring_and_weighting)
- IAALS "Foundations for Practice" (2016) drew more than 24,000 lawyer responses from all 50 states. Characteristics such as integrity, work ethic and resilience were the foundations most often called necessary right out of law school, which the report calls a "character quotient" (search snippets). — [IAALS, The Whole Lawyer and the Character Quotient](https://iaals.du.edu/publications/foundations-practice-whole-lawyer-and-character-quotient); [Survey methodology](https://iaals.du.edu/publications/foundations-practice-survey-overview-and-methodological-approach)

### Inferences
- **MPT-to-LexSim mapping:**
  - legal = skill 2
  - facts = skill 3
  - communication = skill 4
  - ethics = skill 6
  - procedure partly = skill 1 (plan of action) + skill 5
  - time management = skill 5. LexSim could add timed-hearing penalties citing it.
- **Absolute vs relative scoring.** Use absolute criterion-referenced scoring (SQE2 style) for individual feedback. Relative ranking (MPT style) suits only leaderboards and team moot seeding.

- The full IAALS list of foundations and their percentages was not extracted.

## Q3. Moot court scoresheets: Jessup, Vis, Price Media, World Human Rights Moot, US school moots

### Takeaway
Major moots converge on 4–5 criteria: knowledge of law, knowledge of facts, response to questions, organisation/structure, and presentation/delivery. They score on a 50–100 (or 0–100) scale with named bands, and they explicitly tell judges NOT to score the merits of the side argued. Jessup and the World Human Rights Moot publish explicit weights. Law is weighted highest (about 25–35%), questions about 25–35%, facts about 20–25%, and delivery about 10–25%.

### Cited Findings
**Vis Moot (Vienna), 31st edition arbitrator instructions** — [Vis Moot General Arbitrator Instructions (31st)](https://www.vismoot.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/31st-Vis-Moot_General-Arbitrator-Instructions_FINAL.pdf)
- Scale: "Scoring should be done on a scale of 50 to 100 points for each of the oralists ((50 – 59 = needed improvement); (60-74 = good); (75 - 90 = very good); (91 - 100 = excellent))."
- A second, inconsistent band list appears later in the same document: "(50-59 = needed improvement); (60-69 = satisfactory); (70-79 = good); (80-90 = very good); (91-100 = excellent)". Arbitrators are urged to be within the same band "or otherwise generally within 15 marks". The document contradicts itself, so note this when citing.
- Holistic scoring: "The scores of each oralist should be determined on an overall evaluation of his or her presentation." A list of separately graded criteria "has not been adopted".
- No merits: oralists "must not be judged on the merits of the case… An argument that shows a thorough knowledge of the relevant law and the facts may be even more impressive when the student is representing what would seem to be the losing party".
- Halo effect: arbitrators should "judge each oralist independently" and "avoid the 'halo effect'".
- Language: "Mistakes or difficulty in use of the English language should not be penalized" for non-native teams, but there are no extra points either.
- Criteria "to be regarded":
  1. "Organization and Preparation": introduces counsel and client; "introduce the issues and relevant facts clearly"; "present the arguments in an effective sequence"; "persuasive… conclusion"; "If rebuttal is used, is it used effectively?"
  2. "Knowledge of the facts and the law": "Is counsel able to relate the facts to the law so as to make a strong case"; "arguments which are logically plausible and legally tenable"
  3. "Presentation": "appropriately paced, free of mannerisms"; "eye contact"; "balance due deference with a forceful and professional argument"; "poised and tactful under pressure"
  4. "Handling Questions": "answer questions directly and use the opportunity to turn the question to his or her client's advantage"
- Feedback: oral evaluation after the hearing "is often the most valuable aspect of the Moot for the students".

**Jessup (ILSA) oral scoresheet**
- Five categories. Total per oralist between 50 and 100. Category bands are labelled Weak / Average / Strong. Weights found:

  - Questions & Answers, max 25: "Deftly and directly answers questions about facts, law, and policy. Makes strategic concessions and seamlessly ties Q&A into argument"
  - Knowledge of the Facts, max 20
  - Style, Poise & Demeanor, max not found
  - fifth category, likely Organization, max not found
  - Source: search snippets only, because ILSA PDFs return 404/HTML to automated fetch. **[PARTIALLY UNVERIFIED]** — [ILSA 2021 Oral Rounds Scoresheet](https://www.ilsa.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/2021JessupOralRoundsScoresheet_FILLABLE.pdf); [ILSA 2019 scoresheet](https://www.ilsa.org/Jessup/Jessup19/Administrator/JessupOralRoundsScoresheet2019.pdf)
- ILSA "Draft Oral-Round Rubric" dated 9 January 2026, non-binding (snippet): "an overall score below 60 should indicate a fundamental problem…; above a 90 should indicate an award-winning performance". Knowledge of the Law bands are reported as Weak 15–19, Average 20–25. **[UNVERIFIED in full]** — [ILSA 2026 Oral Judging Rubric](https://www.ilsa.org/Jessup/Jessup2026/Admin/2026%20Oral%20Judging%20Rubric.pdf)
- Jessup round points: each judge's ballot gives the winning team round points, and the majority of round points wins the match (snippet). — [ILSA New Judges Guide 2021](https://www.ilsa.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/2021-NewJudgesGuide.pdf)

**Price Media Law Moot (Oxford), 2026–2027 Rules, r. 8.1** — [Price Moot Competition Rules 2026–27 (PDF)](https://www.law.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2026-11/2026-2027%20Competition%20Rules%20Price%20Media%20Law%20Moot%20Court.pdf)
- "Each oralist can be awarded a maximum of one hundred (100) points per oral pleading based on, but not limited to, the following criteria":
  - "Correct legal analysis and its application to facts"
  - "Relevant treaties, relevant customs, case law, regional judgments, legal scholars, other"
  - "Recognition of problems: complete and correct recognition and weighting of problems"
  - "Clarity and logic of argument"
  - "Correct primary and alternative submissions"
  - "Evidence of original thought"
  - "Overall presentation"
  - "Ability to communicate with judges: persuasiveness and fluency"
  - "Rebuttal or sur-rebuttal: ability to respond to opposing side's arguments"
- No per-criterion weights are given. Three judges each score out of 100. Match winner by comparing highest/middle/lowest scores pairwise (1 round point each; 0.5 for a tie) (r. 8.2.1).
- Memorial criteria (r. 6.1.1) also apply to LexSim claim drafting: "Correct legal analysis and its application to facts"; "Quality and extent of research"; "Recognition of problems"; "Correct primary and alternative submissions"; "Clarity and logic of argument"; "Evidence of original thought"; "Overall Presentation: Language, structure, format, citations". Procedural penalties apply separately (r. 6.1.2).

**World Human Rights Moot Court (Univ. of Pretoria, 2023), Appendix D scoring sheet** — [Appendix D Scoring sheet (PDF)](https://www.chr.up.ac.za/images/moots/world_moot/2023/Appendix_D_Scoring_sheet.pdf)

- Scoring guide: Outstanding 90–100%; Excellent 80–89; Very good 70–79; Good 60–69; Average 50–59; Poor 40–49; "Below minimum standard expected" 0–39. Free-text "I found the presentation of Oralist … to be:" box.

- Berkeley McBaine 2025 (100 points, minimum 46). — [2025 McBaine Oral Argument Scoring Sheet](https://law.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/2025-McBaine-Oral-Argument-Scoring-Sheet.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Suggested LexSim moot weights, each piece citable:**
  - legal 30: Jessup law max 30; WHRMC 25
  - rebuttal / answering questions 25: Jessup Q&A 25; McBaine questions 35%

## Q4. Uzbekistan: MoJ Regulation No. 3637 (07.07.2025), university grading rules, judge selection

### Takeaway
Regulation 3637 sets the format and numbers for the advocates' exam: 5 random questions, 0–10 per answer from each commissioner, automatic summation, and a 60% pass mark. It gives only one qualitative criterion: answers must be clear, concise and match the question. LexSim can cite it for the 0–10 scale and the 60% threshold, but its criteria must be borrowed from SQE2 or the moots. The national higher-education grading rule (Order 3069) provides official Uzbek 5-point descriptors.

## Q5. Rubric design principles: analytic vs holistic, descriptors, reliability, feedback

### Takeaway
Analytic rubrics, with criteria scored separately against level descriptors, give higher rater agreement than holistic ones and are what makes "why N points?" answerable. Kappa ≥0.61 ("substantial", Landis & Koch) is the conventional minimum target for AI-vs-expert agreement, with ≥0.81 as "almost perfect". Elite moots such as Vis still use holistic scoring with guidance criteria. A hybrid fits LexSim best: an analytic per-criterion score plus a holistic band label.

### Cited Findings
- In a 2018 study, the analytic condition gave "substantively higher agreement among assessors" than the holistic condition: Cohen's kappa .60 vs .41 (Jönsson & Balan, via search snippet). — [PARE/UMass article](https://openpublishing.library.umass.edu/pare/article/1660/galley/1611/view)
- Jonsson & Svingby (2007) reviewed 75 rubric studies; only 7 reported intra-rater reliability, and most reported inter-rater consensus (as cited in a secondary review). Rubric research has "largely focused on reliability issues". — [Frontiers in Education 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2017.00049/epub)
- Landis & Koch (1977) kappa bands: ≤0 poor; 0.00–0.20 slight; 0.21–0.40 fair; 0.41–0.60 moderate; 0.61–0.80 substantial; 0.81–1.00 almost perfect. The bands are acknowledged as subjective. — [Wikipedia: Cohen's kappa](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohen%27s_kappa); McHugh, "Interrater reliability: the kappa statistic", Biochemia Medica 2012;22(3):276-82 — [PMC3402032](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3402032)
- The Vis Moot treats score divergence greater than 15 points between arbitrators as a signal for deliberation, a practical inter-rater tolerance. — [Vis 31st instructions](https://www.vismoot.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/31st-Vis-Moot_General-Arbitrator-Instructions_FINAL.pdf)
- McBaine instructs judges to anchor at "Good" to "encourage equity and uniformity in scoring". MPT/NCBE tells graders to use the full scale and not compress scores to the middle. — [McBaine 2025](https://law.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/2025-McBaine-Oral-Argument-Scoring-Sheet.pdf); [The Bar Examiner](https://thebarexaminer.ncbex.org/article/june-2016/its-all-relative-mee-and-mpt-grading-that-is-2/)
- Rubric-guided LLM grading of German public-law cases reached QWK up to 0.91 against experts. This is already in `reports/LexSim manbalar asosi.md` and not repeated here.

### Inferences
**Explainability contract for each LexSim score:**
1. criterion name and source (e.g. "Advocacy — 'Make a persuasive argument' (SRA SQE2)")
2. level (A–F → 0–5) with the verbatim descriptor
3. 1–3 transcript quotes as evidence (the Hampel "playback" step)
4. one remedy

**Reliability target.** Before claiming validity, double-mark a calibration set of about 30–50 transcripts with a human mentor. Target weighted kappa / QWK ≥0.61 per criterion, ideally ≥0.81 for the "legal" criterion. Flag any AI-vs-mentor gap over 15/100, following the Vis tolerance.

**Anchoring.** Give the AI judge the descriptor ladder plus anchor exemplars per level. Instruct it to use the full range (NCBE) and not to score merits (Vis).

### Gaps
- The 0.70 kappa threshold often attributed to Jonsson & Svingby could not be verified. Do not cite it.
- McHugh's own interpretation table was not read, and her bands may be stricter than Landis & Koch. Check before citing her for specific cut-offs.
- No empirical study validating automated scoring of oral moot or advocacy performance was found.
