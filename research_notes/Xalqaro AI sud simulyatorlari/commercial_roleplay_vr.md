# Commercial AI roleplay platforms, custom GPTs/bots and VR tools for practising courtroom advocacy, depositions, client interviews and negotiation (state as of 20 Nov 2026)

Verification legend used below. **[V]** means the page was fetched and read on 20 Nov 2026. **[P]** means the URL resolves, but the content is JavaScript-rendered and could not be read, so the details come from search-engine snippets or a third-party page. **[U]** means unverified: the fetch failed (403/404/500/DNS error) or the item was seen only in search results. Unless stated otherwise, every product claim is the vendor's own description. No independent tests were run.

## 1. Which AI roleplay platforms have legal or courtroom use cases? Links and access model

### Takeaway
A cluster of small, self-serve, US-centric legal simulators can be tried today for free or cheaply: MockTrialz, MockTrialOnline, TrialSim, TrialTutor, MootQourt and Depo Prep. The most mature and best-adopted product, AltaClaro/Verbit DepoSim, is sold by demo to firms and law schools only. Among general roleplay vendors, Exec, Yoodli and VirtualSpeech explicitly market legal scenarios (deposition, courtroom, hostile witness, negotiation, client counselling) with free or low-cost entry tiers. No legal-specific offering was found for Mursion, Bodyswaps or Second Nature.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Legal-specific AI simulators, self-serve (sign up and test now)
- **MockTrialz** [V]: https://mocktrialz.com/
  - It is an AI mock-trial platform for law students. "AI judges ask probing Socratic questions", opposing counsel pushes back, and witnesses "respond dynamically". It supports both voice and text ("voice and text-based AI opponents") — [MockTrialz](https://mocktrialz.com/)
  - It covers 34 legal fields (8 core, 17 specialized, 9 emerging), scores points by difficulty (Easy 2, Medium 5, Hard 10) and shows a leaderboard whose sample data is labelled illustrative. Analytics track "argumentation, case law recall, and courtroom presence" — [MockTrialz](https://mocktrialz.com/)
  - Access: "100% free for students", and the first trial needs no signup. Institutional partnerships are arranged by email. It was built by law students, the contact is a Gmail address, and all cases are AI-generated and fictional — [MockTrialz](https://mocktrialz.com/)
- **MockTrialOnline** [V]: https://mocktrialonline.com/
  - It is a browser-based AI mock-trial simulator. The user plays plaintiff's counsel, defense counsel or prosecutor. The AI judge "manages the entire trial", rules on objections and motions and instructs the jury. AI opposing counsel objects and cross-examines. Jury selection runs through voir dire, and the trial ends with jury deliberation and a "vote animation". Input is "voice input or type" — [MockTrialOnline](https://mocktrialonline.com/)
  - The phases run from pretrial motions through voir dire, openings, direct and cross, exhibits and objections to closings and verdict. The site says "all 9 trial phases", while a search listing says "8-phase". It uses the Federal Rules of Evidence or any US state's rules, and offers more than 30 cases, including historical ones such as People v. O.J. Simpson — [MockTrialOnline](https://mocktrialonline.com/); "8-phase" per [MockTrialOnline simulator page via search](https://mocktrialonline.com/mock-trial-simulator) [U]
  - Pricing (promotional prices shown on 20 Nov 2026): Monthly $9.90 (list $19.90), Quarterly $19.90 (list $59.90), Annual $49.90 (list $238.90). Free users "can practice free cases without a credit card". Subscribers get unlimited practice "without spending credits" — [MockTrialOnline pricing](https://mocktrialonline.com/pricing)
- **TrialSim** [V]: https://www.trialsim.io/ (app: https://app.trialsim.io/)
  - It covers moot court oral argument and trial advocacy with "responsive AI simulations". The bench interrupts with judicial questions, and practice is "out loud" — [TrialSim](https://www.trialsim.io/)
  - Its "PRESENCE" scoring has eight dimensions: Preparation, Responsiveness, Execution, Style, Emotional Intelligence, Narrative Control, Courtroom Awareness and Edge — [TrialSim](https://www.trialsim.io/)
  - Access: individuals can "start practicing free with no credit card required". Institutional pricing is "tailored to the program, team, and pilot scope". The company is TrialSim LLC, founded by Kris Charity, Esq., a former Assistant District Attorney. The site says it was "Selected by BAC University in Malaysia for moot court training" and has "reached users across 47 countries" — [TrialSim](https://www.trialsim.io/)
  - A search listing describes "witnesses with personalities, inconsistencies, and cross-exam vulnerabilities" and a three-question demo. A copy is also hosted at trialsim.lovable.app — [TrialSim (lovable mirror, search only)](https://trialsim.lovable.app/) [U]
- **TrialTutor** [V]: https://www.trialtutor.com/
  - It is a "voice-first practice tool for trial skills", currently in limited beta. Users run five-minute drills against an AI witness, AI opposing counsel (who objects) and an AI judge (who rules). Each party has a distinct voice. Case facts are "compiled fresh every turn" so the witness stays consistent. Drills cover cross-examination, objections and foundations, starting with structured scripts and moving to generated questions — [TrialTutor](https://www.trialtutor.com/)
  - Feedback: users can ask "how am I doing?" mid-session. Each session ends with a written report, and the replay is annotated with AI notes on what drew objections and what a stronger question would have been. No numeric score is given — [TrialTutor](https://www.trialtutor.com/)
  - Pricing: the beta is free. Planned prices are $29/month for 60 sessions, Classroom $499/semester for one instructor and 30 students, and Office $3,000/year for up to 15 attorneys. Sign-up is through a beta form, and target users get an invite "within a few minutes". Law schools, clinics and prosecutor/defender offices can apply for free design-partner pilots — [TrialTutor](https://www.trialtutor.com/)
- **MootQourt** [V]: https://www.mootqourt.com/ (how it works: https://www.mootqourt.com/how-it-works)
  - Users upload briefs (PDF/DOCX/TXT, up to 25 MB) or paste summaries, pick a court, and argue out loud or by typing to a single AI judge or a three-judge panel. Judge temperaments are methodical, combative, textualist or consequentialist. An argument clock defaults to the forum's allotment (30 min SCOTUS, 15 min federal appeals) — [MootQourt](https://www.mootqourt.com/how-it-works)
  - The debrief gives six skill scores (responsiveness, command of record, doctrinal precision, handling weak points, concision, composure) plus narrative notes, progress tracking and read-only share links — [MootQourt](https://www.mootqourt.com/how-it-works)
  - Forums: SCOTUS, all 13 circuits, federal district motions, all 50 state high courts, state trial motions, and AAA, JAMS, NLRB and EEOC. On privacy, it does not train on briefs or sessions, and briefs and transcripts are not stored after the session. Pricing is not shown on that page; a sign-up page exists at https://www.mootqourt.com/sign-up — [MootQourt](https://www.mootqourt.com/how-it-works)
- **Depo Prep** [V]: https://depositionprep.io/
  - It offers AI voice mock depositions for preparing clients. "The chatbot mimics a real defense attorney" and is "trained on your client's unique case details". The attorney receives the full transcript, the audio recording and an AI summary — [Depo Prep](https://depositionprep.io/)
  - Price: "Flat fee of $25 per mock deposition" with no monthly fee. A free trial is available ("NO CREDIT CARD NEEDED") — [Depo Prep](https://depositionprep.io/)

#### B. Legal-specific simulators, enterprise or institutional (demo or partnership needed)
- **DepoSim (AltaClaro + Verbit)** [V]: https://www.altaclaro.com/deposim
  - Orchestrated AI agents play the witness, opposing counsel and court reporter. Behaviour is adjustable "from cooperative to hostile, calm to aggressive" inside a "guardrailed" environment built on case facts and witness backstories. It uses TTS/STT, with transcription on Verbit servers and commercial LLMs called via APIs. Feedback is rubric-based (questioning, exhibit handling, objection management, clarity, strategy) and tracks metrics such as follow-up questions and open versus closed questions. The only call to action is "Book a demo" — [AltaClaro DepoSim](https://www.altaclaro.com/deposim)
  - Witness styles include hostile, aggressive, talkative, evasive and reserved. After a session, about 20 additional AI agents evaluate about 40 aspects. In a pilot, six Am Law 100 firms logged 160+ hours and five became paying customers. Vendor-reported satisfaction: 97% found it valuable and 94% would use it again — [Ynet News](https://www.ynetnews.com/tech-and-digital/article/syfurdgmfl)
  - It uses a "lightly scripted framework": the witness has a fixed truth but room to improvise. Users can tune witness cooperation and opposing-counsel aggressiveness — [Thomson Reuters Institute, 15 May 2026](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/legal/ai-simulation-legal-training/)
  - Law-school access: Vanderbilt Law offers DepoSim from fall 2026 through courses and the law library. Trial practice, oral argument, discovery, client interview and negotiation scenarios are planned for "later this year". The article gives no price — [ABA Journal, 10 Sep 2026](https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/vanderbilt-law-students-to-gain-access-to-ai-powered-deposition-simulations)
  - Further adoption and awards appear only in search results: Ogletree Deakins firmwide rollout (23 Sep 2026), Fisher Phillips deployment, the LegalTech Breakthrough "Overall LegalTech Innovation of the Year" award, and launch coverage on LawNext in Feb 2026 — [Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/media-center/press-releases/2026-09-23/ogletree-deakins-launches-firmwide-ai-deposition-training-program-with-altaclaros-deposim/) [U]; [AltaClaro/Fisher Phillips](https://www.altaclaro.com/news/fisher-phillips-deploys-altaclaros-ai-powered-deposition-training-tool-deposim) [U]; [LawNext](https://www.lawnext.com/2026/02/altaclaro-and-verbit-launch-deposim-an-ai-powered-deposition-simulator-for-litigators.html) [U]
- **WytheSmartMoot** [V, minimal]: https://smartmoot.wythe.ai/ — "Practice oral arguments with AI judges trained on your court and case". The page shows only a login link, with no public sign-up or pricing — [SmartMoot](https://smartmoot.wythe.ai/)
- **Legaline "Meta Legal Space" (UAE/MENA)** [V via press; product site U]

#### C. Academic or free public tools (legal)
- **Suffolk Law AI Negotiation** [V]: https://sites.suffolk.edu/ai-negotiation/
  - Users negotiate "either in writing or speaking". Scenarios include a quarry land-purchase dispute, a seminar-speaker contract, and a complex breach-of-warranty settlement, plus a Negotiation Advisor, a Mediation Advisor and a "Disputing Neighbors" mediation (Kenya) — [Suffolk AI Negotiation](https://sites.suffolk.edu/ai-negotiation/)
  - It is free and open to students and the public, with scenario passwords on the site. The bots use "a variety of tactics and strategies used by experienced lawyers", ranging from competitive to collaborative. It was led by Prof. Dwight Golann with the LIT Lab and is experimental — [Suffolk University news, 17 Mar 2025](https://www.suffolk.edu/news-features/news/2025/03/18/20/19/ai-negotiation-tool)
- **Northwestern Pritzker "generative AI clients"** [V] (internal; no public access stated): a React and Flask web app on GPT-4o, integrated with Canvas, with a group-text-style chat. There are two client personas, "Andy" (excitable, overly optimistic) and "Dr. Gold" (methodical, risk-averse), built from compiled backgrounds and traits. The AI does not grade; professors review the transcripts. Inconsistencies in the AI's answers were treated as added realism. It was used with a class of 40 students — [Northwestern IT, 25 Feb 2025](https://www.it.northwestern.edu/about/news-events/2025/northwestern-it-brings-generative-ai-clients-to-the-northwestern-pritzker-school-of-law-classroom.html)
- **Specter (Stanford CodeX)** [V] (access unclear; demo video only): precedent retrieval using "Anthropic's contextual retrieval approach", hundreds of parallel AI-vs-AI lawyer-agent negotiations, and a "real-time voiceBot that attorneys can practice negotiating against" seeded with the most successful simulated strategies — [Stanford CodeX, 17 Oct 2024](https://law.stanford.edu/2024/10/17/adversarial-anticipation-real-time-simulated-negotiation/)
- **WitnessSim (Stanford liftlab)** [V]: a research deposition simulator "driven by controllable legal personas". In blinded comparison, attorneys "did not systematically prefer either original testimony or WitnessSim generated testimony". Witness behaviour changed with question form and attorney intervention without abandoning the persona. It was published 13 Aug 2026 and links to public code and a prototype — [Stanford Law](https://law.stanford.edu/publications/reading-between-the-lines-modeling-and-evaluating-behavioral-realism-in-legal-simulation/)
  - Code: https://github.com/liftlab-SLS/witness-sim-ai4law [V]. Components are `state_engine.py` (question encoding into "pressure/sensitivity scalars", ODE state updates, memory decay, event detection), `prompt_builder.py` (a state-conditioned system prompt) and `batch_sim.py`. It uses 10 behavioural archetypes, a `--docs case.pdf` option to enrich personas, the Anthropic API (`claude-opus-4-5`) and Llama-3.1-8B activations. No license is shown and the repo has 0 stars — [GitHub](https://github.com/liftlab-SLS/witness-sim-ai4law)
  - Prototype: https://atelier-personalities.vercel.app/ [P]. The page title is "Witness Simulator"; the content is JS-rendered and was not readable — [Prototype](https://atelier-personalities.vercel.app/)
  - The TR Institute describes the liftlab tool as having "personality dials" that instructors can set toward forgetfulness, evasiveness or hostility — [Thomson Reuters Institute](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/legal/ai-simulation-legal-training/)
- The Stanford CodeX + Flatiron Law Group genAI M&A negotiation simulator lets users choose junior, senior or partner level — [Artificial Lawyer, Aug 2024 (search only)](https://www.artificiallawyer.com/2024/08/05/stanfords-codex-flatiron-launch-genai-ma-training-simulator/) [U]

### Inferences
- For a hands-on test this week, the most useful products to compare against LexSim's concept are MockTrialz and MockTrialOnline, which are the closest to "pick a side; AI plays judge, opposing counsel, witnesses and jury". MootQourt and TrialSim are the closest to "AI bench questioning", and TrialTutor is the best model for short voice drills with role-distinct voices.
- DepoSim is the market benchmark on evidence and adoption: firm pilots, law-school partnerships, and evaluator agents. It cannot be tried without a demo, so the team should learn from its public design (fixed truth plus improvisation, temperament dials, separate evaluator agents) rather than plan hands-on use.
- Almost every product is built for US common-law practice: FRE objections, voir dire and juries. The only civil-law or post-Soviet analogs found are ChatCourt and LawMatic Trial (Russia) and Legaline (UAE onshore, DIFC, ADGM), which makes them the most relevant design references for an Uzbek-procedure simulator. This draws on the general knowledge that Uzbek procedural codes share post-Soviet roots with Russia's; that point was not researched here.
- General roleplay vendors treat legal work as one vertical. Their legal content is mostly soft-skill scenarios (client conversations, negotiation, hostile witness) rather than procedure-aware courtroom simulation.

## 2. Are there popular custom GPTs, Claude projects or Character.AI-style bots for mock trials or moot court?

### Takeaway
Yes. Several mock-trial and moot GPTs exist in the GPT Store, and two law-school-built GPTs are documented: Suffolk's "Moot a Case based on a Docket" and the Colorado Lawyer examples. Most GPT Store entries are question or problem generators rather than interactive role-players, and their quality and popularity could not be verified because GPT pages are JS-rendered. No public Claude Projects directory for mock trials was found. Character.AI-style courtroom bots are mostly Ace Attorney fan personas with no pedagogy or scoring.

### Inferences
- Most GPT Store "mock trial" GPTs are generators of questions, moot problems or memorials. Only a few, such as the docket moot, Court Simulator and Mock Jury, appear to be interactive role-players, and none show a structured rubric. Their quality is effectively unknown. The best-documented ones, Suffolk and Colorado, are useful but text-only, prone to pacing problems, and capped on free tiers.
- A custom GPT or Claude Project is a fast way to prototype role prompts (judge, witness, objection coach) in a single afternoon, as Pratt's under-one-hour build shows. Classroom-scale use needs a standalone app, because of free-tier exchange limits and the lack of scoring, persistence and procedure control.
- Consumer "courtroom roleplay" bots (Jenova, Shapes, COURT) show demand for gamified courtroom play. Their gamification (ranks, opponent styles, Judge Mode) is a cheap engagement idea, but they lack legal-procedure fidelity and assessment.

- No public directory of Claude Projects exists, and none for mock trials was found. Character.AI's own catalogue could not be searched or verified.
- No GPT or bot was found that is configured for Uzbek or Russian procedure, or marketed in those languages.

## 3. What VR/immersive courtroom training exists?

### Takeaway
VR courtroom offerings fall into three groups. The first is no-code or enterprise VR builders that now bolt on LLM-driven witnesses and judges (SimLab, Virtway), along with a Russian procedural VR trainer sold to universities at about 1.7M RUB (ProgramLab). The second is bespoke university VR courtrooms (Universidad San Sebastián in Chile, the Open University in the UK, and uOttawa and UBC moots). The third is free, government-run witness-familiarisation VR without AI (Scotland, Ireland). None has independent evidence of improved advocacy outcomes, and several efficacy claims are unsourced marketing.

## 4. What feedback/scoring mechanisms do these tools use, and how do they control realism?

### Takeaway
Feedback has converged on four to eight named rubric dimensions, delivered seconds to minutes after a session, often with evidence or annotated replay. Firm-grade tools add separate evaluator agents and objective question metrics, such as open versus closed questions. Realism is controlled by combining a fixed case record ("fixed truth") with persona or temperament dials, uploaded case documents, jurisdiction-specific rules, and, in the more robust designs, a deterministic procedure engine that keeps the LLM from "running the trial" on its own.

### Cited Findings

#### Scoring and feedback mechanisms
- MockTrialOnline uses four dimensions in the verdict: argumentation strength, evidence use, objection accuracy and courtroom expression, plus "improvement tips". Objection drills give instant AI-judge feedback — [MockTrialOnline](https://mocktrialonline.com/)
- MootQourt uses six skill scores (responsiveness, command of record, doctrinal precision, handling weak points, concision, composure) plus narrative notes and progress tracking — [MootQourt](https://www.mootqourt.com/how-it-works)
- TrialSim's "PRESENCE" has eight dimensions (Preparation, Responsiveness, Execution, Style, Emotional Intelligence, Narrative Control, Courtroom Awareness, Edge) — [TrialSim](https://www.trialsim.io/)
- In VirtualSpeech, admins choose five scoring criteria per scenario. Scores arrive "within seconds" with evidence for each (example overall score 7.1), followed by a two-way debrief with an AI Coach — [VirtualSpeech](https://virtualspeech.com/roleplay-sessions)
- In Yoodli, "The AI scores performance against criteria you define", and custom rubrics are available in the Business and Enterprise tiers — [Yoodli legal](https://yoodli.ai/blog/ai-roleplay-training-for-legal-teams); [Yoodli pricing](https://yoodli.ai/pricing)
- Exec lists "Custom Rubrics", "Custom Scorecards" and "Call Scoring" as features — [Exec pricing](https://www.exec.com/pricing)
- DepoSim feedback is rubric-based (questioning, exhibit handling, objection management, clarity, overall strategy) and tracks metrics such as follow-up questions and open versus closed questions — [AltaClaro](https://www.altaclaro.com/deposim). About 20 evaluator agents assess about 40 aspects after each session — [Ynet News](https://www.ynetnews.com/tech-and-digital/article/syfurdgmfl)
- Legaline reports score argumentation, procedural compliance and objection handling. It adds a leaderboard and recorded sessions, and a licensed practitioner can take the judge's seat and score live — [Middle East AI News](https://www.middleeastainews.com/p/legaline-launches-menas-first-ai)
- TrialTutor gives no numeric score. Instead it offers mid-session "how am I doing?", an end-of-session report and a replay annotated with what drew objections and what a stronger question would have been — [TrialTutor](https://www.trialtutor.com/)
- Depo Prep provides a transcript, the audio and an AI summary for the attorney to review with the client — [Depo Prep](https://depositionprep.io/)
- ProgramLab offers a training mode (hints after each action plus a report) and an exam mode (no hints, limited wrong actions, points). Its teacher report analyses errors with citations to procedural-code articles — [ProgramLab](https://pl-llc.ru/catalog/ekonomika-i-predprinimatelstvo/virtualnye-trenazhery-dlya-spetsialistov-yuridicheskogo-profilya-/virtualnyy-vr-trenazher-protsedurnyy-simulyator-sudebnogo-zasedaniya-po-grazhdanskim-delam/)
- In ChatCourt, "the teacher grades the student's conduct within the process, not the case outcome" — [Habr](https://habr.com/ru/articles/1108458/)
- In the Northwestern tool, the AI does not grade; professors grade the submitted client-interview transcripts — [Northwestern IT](https://www.it.northwestern.edu/about/news-events/2025/northwestern-it-brings-generative-ai-clients-to-the-northwestern-pritzker-school-of-law-classroom.html)
- SimLab's AI judge rejects improper questions "with clear justification", and its AI speech analysis covers pace, eye contact, filler words and argument structure — [SimLab](https://www.simlab-soft.com/use_cases/simlab-vr-law-court.aspx)
- Gamified scoring: MockTrialz awards points by difficulty and has a leaderboard, and COURT uses XP and ranks with unlock thresholds — [MockTrialz](https://mocktrialz.com/); [COURT](https://gguruji1985.github.io/Law-based-game/)
- Design principle from the industry: "clear learning goals with a rubric, realistic unpredictability, and specific feedback". AltaClaro's Jeremy Liles warns that AI tools without rubric-based simulation infrastructure yield "productivity without judgment" — [Thomson Reuters Institute](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/legal/ai-simulation-legal-training/)

- **Persona and temperament dials**:
  - DepoSim: from cooperative to hostile and from calm to aggressive — [AltaClaro](https://www.altaclaro.com/deposim)
  - Stanford liftlab: dials toward forgetfulness, evasiveness or hostility — [TR Institute](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/legal/ai-simulation-legal-training/)
  - Legaline: preset honesty, reliability and composure, and each witness withholds one fact — [Solicitors Journal](https://www.solicitorsjournal.com/sjarticle/legaline-launches-ai-courtroom-simulator)
  - SimLab: hostile, nervous or evasive avatars — [SimLab](https://www.simlab-soft.com/use_cases/simlab-vr-law-court.aspx)
  - MootQourt: methodical, combative, textualist or consequentialist judges — [MootQourt](https://www.mootqourt.com/how-it-works)

  - ProgramLab: AI participants act "within the scenario logic", using teacher-built block-diagram scenarios — [ProgramLab](https://pl-llc.ru/catalog/ekonomika-i-predprinimatelstvo/virtualnye-trenazhery-dlya-spetsialistov-yuridicheskogo-profilya-/virtualnyy-vr-trenazher-protsedurnyy-simulyator-sudebnogo-zasedaniya-po-grazhdanskim-delam/)

### Inferences — design lessons for LexSim (an Uzbek courtroom simulator: student picks a role; AI plays judge, opposing counsel and witnesses)
1. **Put procedure in code, and let the LLM supply the words.** Model the stages of the relevant Uzbek procedure code as a deterministic state machine, as ChatCourt and ProgramLab do. The engine decides whose turn it is and which actions are legal; the LLM generates speech within those options. Use low temperature for the judge (about 0.2) and slightly higher for parties, as ChatCourt does.
2. **Rebuild role context every turn** rather than trusting chat memory. ChatCourt's seven-section per-role table and TrialTutor's "facts compiled fresh every turn" are cheap ways to stop witnesses contradicting the case file.
3. **Witness design = fixed truth + dials + one hidden fact.** Combine DepoSim's "fixed truth, room to improvise" with Legaline's honesty and composure presets and its "each witness withholds one fact". The hidden fact doubles as an objective scoring hook: did the student elicit it?
4. **Offer judge temperaments.** MootQourt's four temperaments show that judge style is a cheap, high-value variety lever. For LexSim this could be a strict-on-procedure judge versus an actively questioning judge; mapping to Uzbek judicial practice needs local validation.
5. **Let any role be human or AI.** Legaline and ProgramLab allow role switching, and COURT has a Judge Mode. LexSim's "pick a role" already matches; adding "classmate vs classmate with AI judge" is a low-cost multiplayer extension.
6. **Score process, not verdict, using four to eight named Uzbek-language rubric dimensions with evidence quotes.** This follows ChatCourt, MootQourt, MockTrialOnline, VirtualSpeech and PRESENCE. Add a post-session evaluator pass separate from the role-play agents, as DepoSim does, plus ProgramLab-style citations of the specific procedural-code article violated, which is strongly differentiating for civil-law training. Offer training mode (hints) and exam mode (points, no hints), and keep a teacher dashboard and a human-judge override (Legaline).
7. **Make it voice-capable, with distinct voices per role**, as TrialTutor does. Advocacy tools increasingly default to speech.
8. **Pace explicitly.** GPT prototypes rush through trials (Colorado Lawyer), so the engine should gate stage transitions.
9. **Adopt privacy defaults**: no training on uploads and no transcript retention by default (MootQourt; Exec says it never trains on customer data). Warn against uploading privileged real-case files (Colorado Lawyer).
10. **Build measurement in.** The field lacks outcome evidence (TR Institute), so pre/post rubric scores and a WitnessSim-style blinded realism check with local practitioners would be a credible differentiator at a hackathon and beyond.

### Gaps
- Exact scoring algorithms (weights, LLM-as-judge prompts, calibration against human graders) are not published by any vendor.
- No vendor publishes inter-rater reliability between AI scores and human faculty scores.

## 5. Price and access (free tier, trial, enterprise-only) and supported languages (especially Russian and Uzbek)

### Takeaway
Self-serve legal simulators are free or cheap: free tiers or betas, with paid plans from about $10/month (MockTrialOnline promotional price) to $29/month (TrialTutor, planned) or $25 per mock deposition (Depo Prep). Firm-grade tools (DepoSim, SmartMoot, Mursion, Second Nature, SimLab) require a demo or sales contact. On language: no US legal simulator states non-English support. Russian is supported by Yoodli, VirtualSpeech, Hyperbound and Outdoo, and by Russian-native tools (ChatCourt, LawMatic Trial, ProgramLab). Uzbek appears only in Outdoo's voice roleplay list, and no Uzbek-language legal simulator was found.

### Inferences
- **Uzbek is an open niche.** No legal simulator found, commercial or academic, operates in Uzbek, and among general roleplay platforms only Outdoo lists Uzbek (voice only, with a quality caveat). LexSim's Uzbek-first design is therefore a genuine differentiator. The team should benchmark Uzbek speech recognition and TTS quality early, because that is where the vendors themselves hedge (Outdoo).
- **Russian works as a benchmark and fallback language.** Yoodli, VirtualSpeech and Hyperbound let the team experience multilingual roleplay quality in Russian today. A bilingual Uzbek/Russian setting may suit Uzbekistan's legal-education audience; whether Russian is still common in Uzbek legal education is a user-research question not covered here.
- **Keep every layer in one language.** Bodyswaps' split, with the avatar in the chosen language but feedback only in English or French, and Yoodli's split, with the practice language separate from the UI language and untranslated admin content, show the failure modes. LexSim should deliver role dialogue, rubric feedback and procedural citations all in Uzbek.
- **Access model**: the free-first pattern (no-signup first trial, free student tier, credit caps) dominates student-facing tools (MockTrialz, MockTrialOnline, TrialSim, Legaline). Firm-facing tools monetize through demos and seats. A LexSim hackathon demo should allow a no-signup first case.
- **Model sovereignty**: the ChatCourt precedent suggests checking whether Uzbek universities can use foreign LLM APIs, and under what data-protection rules, before committing to a single provider. This was not researched.
- **Closest analog stack for a hackathon**: a ChatCourt-style deterministic stage engine, plus WitnessSim-style persona and state conditioning (public code), plus MootQourt or VirtualSpeech-style rubric debriefs, plus TrialTutor-style per-role voices.

### Gaps
- The supported languages of the US legal simulators were not stated, and none were tested in Russian or Uzbek prompts during this research.
- Mursion's and Second Nature's language lists could not be verified first-hand (the Mursion page returned 404 and Second Nature's support domain failed DNS).
- Exec's named language list was not found.
- Uzbek speech-to-text and TTS providers, and their quality, were not researched here; they are a critical dependency for LexSim's voice mode.
- Whether Legaline's Meta Legal Space supports Arabic, English or Russian is not stated in coverage. The team could ask the vendor, since its UAE civil-law and arbitration focus makes it the closest commercial analog.
