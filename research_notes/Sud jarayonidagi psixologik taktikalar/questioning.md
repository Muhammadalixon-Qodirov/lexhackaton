# Lawful cross-examination and questioning techniques for the LexSim AI opponent (Uzbek economic court)

How to use these notes: the AI opponent may only use techniques marked LAWFUL. Each technique ends with an ETHICAL LINE showing where it becomes misconduct. The running example is a **lease (ijara) rent-debt case**. The lessor sues for unpaid rent. The lessee says the premises were never handed over because the **acceptance-transfer act (qabul-topshirish dalolatnomasi) was not signed**. However, the lessee's director or accountant **signed monthly invoices (hisob-faktura) / acts of completed services (bajarilgan ishlar dalolatnomasi)** for "rent of premises" for several months, and in some versions made partial payments.

Uzbek terms are suggestions in Latin-script Uzbek. They are not established doctrinal terms unless stated.

## 1. Irving Younger's "Ten Commandments of Cross-Examination": rules, reasoning, use against an unprepared respondent

### Takeaway
Younger's 1975 rules all serve one goal: **control of the adverse witness**. They work by asking short, leading, one-fact questions whose answers are already known, and by stopping before the "one question too many". Critics (Roy Black, Temple's Edward Ohlbaum) call them a useful foundation but "significantly incomplete" and not absolute. In an Uzbek economic court the judge controls the hearing, so commandment 8 ("don't let the witness explain") cannot be enforced the way it is in the US.

### Cited Findings
- Younger, a Cornell law professor, set out the Ten Commandments in a 1975 lecture, and they became the standard advice on cross-examination. — [Holland & Knight / Dan Small podcast](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/media-entities/2024/08/podcast-the-ten-commandments-of-cross-examination); [Wikipedia: Irving Younger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Younger); [Stetson Advocacy Resource Center](https://www2.stetson.edu/advocacy-resource-center/index.php/2023/09/19/irving-youngers-10-commandments-of-cross-examination) (page could not be fetched; seen in search results only)
- The ten rules and their stated reasoning, as summarized by Roy Black. — [Roy Black, "Irving Younger's Ungodly Ten Commandments"](https://www.royblack.com/blog/irving-youngers-ungodly-ten-commandments/)
  1. **Be brief.** The fact-finder absorbs only a few points, so make only a few that bear on reliability.
  2. **Use plain words.** Short, simple questions with no filler modifiers that give the witness room to quibble.
  3. **Ask only leading questions.** The question suggests the answer, which gives control. Black: open "why" questions are "too dangerous and not worth the risk."
  4. **Be prepared / never ask a question you don't know the answer to.** If you don't know the answer, you get the harmful one.
  5. **Listen** to the answers.
  6. **Don't quarrel with the witness.** The fact-finder sides with the witness.
  7. **Avoid repetition.** Don't let the witness repeat and strengthen the direct testimony.
  8. **Don't permit the witness to explain.** Explanations wander and add damage.
  9. **Limit questioning / avoid the "one question too many."** Younger's example is Lincoln's nose-biting case.
  10. **Save the ultimate point for summation.** Don't ask the final "so…" question; argue it in closing.
- Black's critiques. Brevity "keeps you safe but also denies you any rewards". Over-preparation "allows you to anticipate most of the answers". On rule 8, judges rarely enforce it and asking them for help looks like whining, so repeat the question until it is answered. "Younger fails to allow for the third party involved in the examination – the judge." The real rule 9 is "know when to quit". On rule 10, "Cross examination is when the jury is listening carefully so take advantage of that fact." Black's overall view: "There are no commandments handed down from on high." — [Roy Black](https://www.royblack.com/blog/irving-youngers-ungodly-ten-commandments/)
- Temple (Ohlbaum) calls the commandments a foundation but "significantly incomplete". It says they omit storyboards (fitting evidence into a story frame), exhibits and imagery, primacy and recency inside each "chapter" and in the order of chapters, adjustments for special witnesses (experts: "death by a thousand cuts"), and redirect. Quoting Pozner & Dodd: "It is far safer to let the jury reach its own conclusion." — [Temple Law AER blog](https://law.temple.edu/aer/?p=634)
- Other revisits: "The Ten Commandments of Cross-Examination Revisited" (Orrick, 1994) and Dan Small's update. — [Orrick PDF](https://media.orrick.com/Media%20Library/public/files/insights/pre%202010/1994-12-01-the-ten-commandments-of-cross-examination-revisited.pdf); [H&K Dan Small PDF](https://www.hklaw.com:443/files/Uploads/Documents/Articles/DanSmall/TenCommandments.pdf) (not fetched; seen in search results)

## 2. Technique catalog: leading questions, funnels, yes/no traps, one question too many, looping, chapter method, lock-in, impeachment, admissions, imprecise wording, reframing, compound and false-dilemma questions, and which are objectionable

### Takeaway
The core lawful toolkit is the **chapter method**: short leading questions, one fact each, building to a goal fact. Supporting tools are **locking in** testimony, **impeachment** (commit, credit, confront) with prior statements and documents, **looping** with fair labels, and harvesting **admissions**. Questions become objectionable when they are **compound**, **argumentative**, **assume facts not in evidence**, **misstate or misquote** testimony or exhibits, **harass**, or, under US-style rules, are **leading on direct**.

### Cited Findings
**Leading questions and scope**
- FRE 611(c): "Leading questions should not be used on direct examination except as necessary to develop the witness's testimony". Courts ordinarily allow them on cross and when a party calls a hostile witness or an adverse party. FRE 611(a): the court controls mode and order so as to determine the truth, avoid wasting time, and "protect witnesses from harassment or undue embarrassment". 611(b): cross is limited to the subject matter of direct plus credibility. — [LII, FRE 611](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_611)

**Chapter method and looping (Pozner & Dodd)**
- The chapter method organizes cross into "a series of small examinations (chapters) on individual topics", each with its own purpose. It is chapter 2 of *Cross-Examination: Science and Techniques*. — [LexisNexis store page](https://store.lexisnexis.com/products/crossexamination-science-and-techniques-skuusSku6893); [Law Society NSW material (airdrive)](https://airdrive.eventsair.com/eventsairaueprod/production-lansw-public/4ccc7bbfb6f14c718226445e36071b8a) (seen in search snippets only)
- Looping means finding "a descriptive but fair label that is consistent with the cross-examiner's theory" and repeating it so the fact-finder remembers it. The book's loops chapter covers loop formulas, emotional words, and using loops with experts and to label exhibits. — [Dodd, "Loops, double loops" (IADC PDF)](https://www.iadclaw.org/assets/1/19/33_2_Dodd_-_Loops_double_loops.pdf); [vLex, Chapter 10 Looping](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/chapter-10-looping-1037055066) (both seen in search snippets; full text not fetched)
- Primacy and recency should govern each question, each chapter, and the order of chapters. — [Temple AER](https://law.temple.edu/aer/?p=634)

**Lock-in and impeachment by prior inconsistent statement**
- Dan Small's "Three C's" are **Commit** (lock the witness into the current testimony), **Credit** (build up the reliability of the prior statement), and **Confront**. — [H&K podcast, Three C's](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/media-entities/2025/06/podcast-the-three-cs-for-addressing-prior-inconsistent-statements); [UH Impeachment Chart](https://law.uh.edu/blakely/trial-advocacy-Non-litigators/Impeachment%20Chart.pdf) ("CREDIT Build up credibility of prior statement. Establish oath, deposition or other procedures, opportunity to change prior statement")
- The prior statement must actually be inconsistent and must be the witness's own. — [UNC School of Government](https://www.sog.unc.edu/blogs/nc-criminal-law/impeachment-prior-inconsistent-statement-0)
- Attempted impeachment is "frequently ineffective" unless the inconsistency is clear and the confrontation leaves the witness no room to wiggle. — [H&K, Basic points for impeaching (PDF)](https://www.hklaw.com/files/uploads/documents/articles/dansmall/basicpointsforimpeaching.pdf) (seen in search snippet)
- When to impeach (start, end, or in chronological order) depends on your purpose. — [Temple AER](https://law.temple.edu/aer/?p=2949)

**Objectionable forms**
- **Assumes facts not in evidence**: the question presumes unproved facts, as in "When did you stop beating your wife?". **Compound**: two questions in one. **Argumentative**: not designed to elicit facts but to argue the case. It is generally improper but sometimes tolerated on cross. **Misstates or misquotes** testimony or evidence ("counsel is testifying"). — [Wikipedia: Objection (US law)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Objection_(United_States_law)); [H&K checklist of common objections (PDF)](https://www.hklaw.com/files/Uploads/Documents/Articles/DanSmall/checklist-of-common-ob.pdf); [King County, Williams objections](https://kingcounty.gov/-/media/courts/superior-court/docs/williams-objections.ashx) (search snippets)

**Ethical line**
- Questions "intended to insult or annoy" or "needlessly offensive in form" are forbidden even if relevant (Singapore). — [Law Society of Singapore v Wong Sin Yee](https://sg.vlex.com/vid/law-society-of-singapore-842793661) (snippet)
- The right to test evidence "does not extend to insulting or intimidating a witness" (Lord Carloway, Scotland). — [Law Society of Scotland](https://www.lawscot.org.uk/news-and-events/legal-news/defence-questioning-must-respect-victims-rights-carloway-warns/)
- Malaysian advocates' rule 13: guard against putting questions meant only to insult or annoy. — [Malaysian Bar](https://www.malaysianbar.org.my/article/members/laws-bc-rulings-and-practice-directions/legal-profession-practice-and-etiquette-rules-1978/rule-13-advocate-and-solicitor-to-guard-against-insulting-or-annoying-questions-)

### The catalog (techniques built from the cited principles and adapted to the lease case. Example lines are original illustrations, not quotations.)

Hearing stages in Uzbek economic court (EPC Arts. 168, 173): **[E]** explanations of persons participating (tushuntirishlar); **[Q]** questions to the other side or a witness during examination of evidence; **[D]** examination of documentary evidence; **[C]** judicial debates (sud muzokaralari) and reply.

#### T1. Leading question (*Yo'naltiruvchi savol*)
- **What:** The question contains the answer and invites "yes".
- **Example:** "Your accountant Ms. K. signed the invoice of 31 January for 'rent of premises', correct?"
- **Stage:** [Q], [D].
- **Signals:** Starts with a statement and ends with "correct?/to'g'rimi?/shunday emasmi?". The questioner is testifying, not asking.
- **Counter:** Answer the true part and correct the false part in one sentence. If the opponent leads its *own* witness on a contested point, ask the court to have the question rephrased. The Uzbek EPC has no express ban (see section 3), so the argument is that the answer is suggested and has low evidential value (Arts. 89, 88).
- **Student mistakes:** Saying a bare "yes" to a mixed true/false premise. Objecting to leading questions on cross as if they were prohibited.
- **Ethical line:** Lawful. It becomes improper when the embedded "fact" is known to be false or is not in the file (see T11, T12).

#### T2. Closed-question funnel / staircase (*Voronka*, *Zinapoya savollar*)
- **What:** A chain of small undeniable yes-questions that leads to a conclusion the witness can no longer deny. This is the chapter method in action.
- **Example:** "You had a contract dated 1 Jan?" / "The contract names the premises at X?" / "From February you received invoices monthly?" / "You signed each?" / "You never sent a written objection?" / "You paid 20 million in March with the purpose 'rent for February'?" The goal fact is never asked; it is argued in [C].
- **Stage:** [Q], [D].
- **Signals:** Every question is trivially true and the direction is obvious. The opponent avoids the final question.
- **Counter:** Spot where the staircase leads and break it *at the step that is not neutral*. "I signed invoices as accounting documents. Signing was not acceptance of the premises; the contract clause 3.2 requires a bilateral act." Then make the full explanation in [E] or [C].
- **Student mistakes:** Agreeing to each step and then contradicting the conclusion, which looks evasive. Or refusing to admit obvious facts, which destroys credibility.
- **Ethical line:** Lawful.

#### T3. Yes/no trap (*"Ha yoki yo'q" tuzog'i*)
- **What:** A demand for a binary answer to a question that honestly needs qualifying.
- **Example:** "Yes or no: did you occupy the premises?" (when the lessee had partial access only).
- **Stage:** [Q].
- **Signals:** "Just yes or no."
- **Counter:** "Hurmatli sud, the question cannot be answered truthfully with yes or no; I ask permission to answer fully." Art. 89 EPC makes party explanations evidence to be examined, and Art. 54 obliges a witness to give *truthful* testimony, not truncated answers.
- **Student mistakes:** Giving in. Or giving a speech instead of a short "Partly. Here is how."
- **Ethical line:** Lawful as control. Improper if used to obtain an answer the questioner knows is misleading and then presented to the court as a full admission.

#### T4. "One question too many" (*Ortiqcha savol*), as a trap and as a student error
- **What:** Asking the final "why/so" lets the witness explain everything away. The AI opponent avoids it. When the *student* examines, the opponent's witness benefits if the student asks it.
- **Example of student error:** After good admissions, the student asks the lessor's manager, "So why didn't you insist on signing the act?" The manager answers: "Because your director said the keys were enough and asked us to send invoices."
- **Stage:** [Q].
- **Counter (as examiner):** Stop at the admission and argue the inference in [C] (Younger 9 and 10; Pozner & Dodd: let the fact-finder conclude).
- **Ethical line:** Not an ethics issue.

#### T5. Looping (*Yorliq takrorlash / ilmoq*)
- **What:** Fix a fair label consistent with your theory and repeat it inside later questions.
- **Example:** "...the *six signed invoices*..." / "After the *six signed invoices*, you made a payment?" / "Before the *six signed invoices* you never wrote that the premises were not handed over?"
- **Stage:** [Q], [C].
- **Signals:** The same phrase recurs, sometimes with loaded adjectives.
- **Counter:** Accept accurate labels. Reject loaded ones out loud: "They are tax invoices, not acts of acceptance." Introduce your own counter-label in [C] ("an act that was never signed").
- **Student mistakes:** Adopting the opponent's label in your own speech.
- **Ethical line:** The label must be *fair* (Dodd). A label that misdescribes a document (e.g. calling an invoice "your acceptance act") becomes mischaracterization of evidence (T12).

#### T6. Chapter method (*Bo'limli so'roq*)
- **What:** Split the cross into self-contained topics, each ending on a goal fact. Order them by primacy and recency.
- **Example chapters:** (1) the contract and the premises; (2) the invoices; (3) the payments and their purpose wording; (4) the absence of any objection letter; (5) the authority of the signer.
- **Stage:** Planning for [Q]. The final chapter is chosen to be remembered.
- **Signals:** Topic jumps announced with "Now about the payments...".
- **Counter:** Keep your own map of which chapters harm you. Prepare a one-sentence answer for each. Ask the court to let you complete an answer before the next chapter starts.
- **Ethical line:** Lawful.

#### T7. Lock-in before confrontation (Commit, Credit, Confront) (*Qotirib qo'yish -> ishonchlilikni oshirish -> qarama-qarshi qo'yish*)
- **What:** First get the witness to repeat and commit to the current version. Then build up the reliability of the earlier statement or document. Then confront.
- **Example:** Commit: "Today you say you never had access to the premises?" "Never?" "Not even once?" Credit: "On 15 March your company sent a letter, signed by your director, on letterhead, with an outgoing number?" Confront: "Please read the second paragraph: 'Due to a leaking roof in the leased premises, our goods were damaged...'"
- **Stage:** [Q] together with [D].
- **Signals:** Repeated "never?/hech qachon?" and a document held back.
- **Counter:** Avoid absolute words ("never", "hech qachon") unless certain. If confronted, explain the inconsistency honestly and briefly. If the document was not disclosed beforehand, object under **Art. 68 EPC** (parties may rely only on evidence the other participants were acquainted with in advance) and ask to see it.
- **Student mistakes:** Over-committing. Denying one's own company's letter.
- **Ethical line:** The prior statement must truly be inconsistent and be the witness's own (UNC). Reading a selective extract that changes its meaning is misleading the court.

#### T8. Impeachment by documents (*Hujjat bilan inkor etish*)
- **What:** Contradict oral testimony with the party's own documents: invoices, payment orders, letters, emails, messenger messages (digital evidence, Art. 168 EPC).
- **Example:** "Payment order no. 45, purpose of payment: 'ijara haqi, fevral 2025'. Your bank, your signature?"
- **Stage:** [D], [Q].
- **Counter:** Explain the document's purpose and limits (e.g. "an advance against the future handover"). Challenge authenticity or the signer's authority. Ask the court to examine the original.
- **Ethical line:** Using a document known to be forged or altered is misconduct and can be a crime. Hiding a document the court requested triggers the adverse presumption in **Art. 68 EPC**: the withheld information is presumed to be against that party and treated as admitted.

#### T9. Harvesting admissions against interest (*E'tirofni yig'ish*)
- **What:** Obtain clear admissions of facts that help the opponent, then cite them in [C].
- **Example:** "You agree that the 20 million paid in March was not returned to you and you did not ask for it back?"
- **Stage:** [E], [Q].
- **Counter:** Know **Art. 89 EPC**. A party's admission of a fact is *not binding* on the court. The court may treat it as established only if it matches the case circumstances and there is no doubt that it was made without deception, violence, threat or **misleading (chalg'itish)**, or to hide the truth. If an admission was obtained through a misleading question, say so on the record and ask the court not to treat it as established.
- **Student mistakes:** Conceding legal conclusions ("yes, we owe") instead of facts.
- **Ethical line:** Art. 89 itself names misleading as a vitiating factor, so an admission engineered by a false premise is both unethical and evidentially weak.

#### T10. Exploiting imprecise wording (*Noaniq so'zni ilish*)
- **What:** Seize on vague words ("we used", "sort of", "approximately", "I think") and pin them to the strongest meaning.
- **Example:** "You said 'we stored a few things there'. So your goods were inside the premises from February?"
- **Stage:** [E], [Q].
- **Counter:** Use precise words. If you misspoke, correct it immediately: "I correct myself: we had one-day access on 3 February for inspection only."
- **Ethical line:** Lawful if the paraphrase is fair. Restating the words as something not said is misquoting (T12).

#### T11. Reframing / recharacterizing the fact inside the question (*Faktni qayta talqin qilish*)
- **What:** Put the questioner's characterization into the question ("handover", "acceptance", "use").
- **Example:** "After you *accepted* the premises in January, when did you first complain?"
- **Stage:** [Q].
- **Signals:** A legal conclusion presented as a neutral fact. Often also *assumes facts not in evidence*.
- **Counter:** "Hurmatli sud, the question presumes acceptance, which is exactly the disputed issue. I ask that it be rephrased." Answer: "I did not accept the premises."
- **Ethical line:** Improper when it states as established a fact the court has not found. Under **Art. 173 EPC** participants may not rely in the debates on circumstances not established by the court or on evidence not examined at the hearing.

#### T12. Misquoting or mischaracterizing testimony or evidence (*Ko'rsatuvni buzib iqtibos keltirish*): OBJECTIONABLE / MISCONDUCT
- **What:** "Earlier you said you received the keys..." when the witness said no such thing. Or describing an invoice as an "acceptance act".
- **Counter:** Object at once: "Hurmatli sud, the witness did not say that. I ask the court to check the protocol / audio record." Ask that the correct wording be recorded in the minutes.
- **Ethical line:** This is misleading the court. The AI opponent must **never** do this. Under US rules it draws a "misstates the evidence" objection ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Objection_(United_States_law))). In Uzbekistan it breaches the duty to use procedural rights in good faith (Art. 42 EPC).

#### T13. Compound question (*Qo'shma savol*): OBJECTIONABLE
- **Example:** "Did you sign the invoices and agree that the premises were handed over?"
- **Counter:** "There are two questions. To the first: yes. To the second: no." Or ask the court to split it.
- **Ethical line:** Improper form. The AI should use it only in "trainer mode" so students learn to split it.

#### T14. False dilemma (*Soxta tanlov*)
- **Example:** "Either the premises were handed over, or you signed six false invoices. Which one?"
- **Counter:** Name the third option: "Neither. The invoices were signed by mistake by an accountant without authority, and the contract makes the act the only proof of handover."
- **Ethical line:** Argumentative. A fair point to argue in [C], but improper as a question meant to force a concession.

#### T15. Argumentative question (*Bahsli savol*): OBJECTIONABLE
- **Example:** "Don't you think it's dishonest to sign invoices and then refuse to pay?"
- **Counter:** "Hurmatli sud, this is not a question about facts but an argument; I ask the court not to require an answer." Under Art. 167 the presiding judge maintains order.
- **Ethical line:** Improper. Insulting framing approaches harassment ([Singapore](https://sg.vlex.com/vid/law-society-of-singapore-842793661); [Scotland](https://www.lawscot.org.uk/news-and-events/legal-news/defence-questioning-must-respect-victims-rights-carloway-warns/)).

#### T16. Assumes facts not in evidence (*Isbotlanmagan faktga tayangan savol*): OBJECTIONABLE
- **Example:** "Since you subleased the premises to a third company, how much did you earn?" (no evidence of a sublease).
- **Counter:** "There is no evidence of a sublease in the file. I object to the premise." Rely on Art. 68 (only disclosed evidence) and Art. 173 (no reliance on unestablished facts).
- **Ethical line:** Improper. An insinuation with no factual basis is misconduct.

#### T17. Source-of-knowledge probe (*Bilim manbaini so'rash*)
- **What:** Ask the witness how they know. Under **Art. 88 EPC**, a witness's information is *not evidence* if the witness cannot name its source.
- **Example:** "You say the keys were handed over on 10 January. Were you personally present? Who told you?"
- **Stage:** [Q] of witnesses.
- **Counter (when the student's witness is probed):** Call only witnesses with first-hand knowledge. Prepare them to state the source.
- **Ethical line:** Lawful. It is a strong Uzbek-specific tool, and the AI opponent should use it.

#### T18. Authority and signer probe (*Vakolat tekshiruvi*)
- **Example:** "Ms. K. is the accountant? Does she have a power of attorney to accept property? Is it in the file?"
- **Stage:** [Q], [D].
- **Counter:** Prepare evidence of authority or ratification (subsequent payments by the director).
- **Ethical line:** Lawful.

#### T19. Pace and silence (*Sur'at va sukut*)
- **What:** A rapid series of short questions, or a deliberate pause after an answer that tempts the witness to add more.
- **Counter:** Answer, then stop. Ask for the question to be repeated if needed.
- **Ethical line:** Lawful. Intimidating tone or shouting crosses into harassment, and the presiding judge may warn and then remove the person (Art. 167 EPC).

### Inferences
- For training value, the AI opponent should run T1 to T11 and T17 to T19 by default as lawful moves. T12 to T16 should appear only in a flagged "objection practice" mode, where the AI deliberately commits an improper form and scores whether the student objects correctly through the court.

## 3. Translation to Uzbek and CIS economic-court hearings (questions through the court)

### Takeaway
The Uzbek Economic Procedure Code (EPC, lex.uz act 3523891, current consolidated text) gives participants a **right to ask questions** and a duty of **good-faith** use of rights (Art. 42). Witnesses must answer questions from the judge and the participants (Art. 54). However, the Code **contains no Anglo-American cross-examination rules**: no ban on leading questions, no direct/cross order, and no express list of objections. In the text I searched, I found no rule on the judge disallowing questions. Students must therefore frame "objections" as **motions or statements to the court** (Art. 42 "ariza/iltimosnoma", decided under Art. 169) and anchor them in Arts. 68, 88, 89, 167 and 173.

### Cited Findings (all from the Uzbek EPC, [lex.uz/ru/acts/3523891](https://lex.uz/ru/acts/3523891); text is in Uzbek Cyrillic, translations are mine)
- **Art. 42**: Participants have the right to "participate in examining evidence, **ask questions**, file motions, make statements, give oral and written explanations to the court... and object to other participants' motions and arguments". They "must use all procedural rights **in good faith (insofli)**".
- **Art. 43**: The parties enjoy equal procedural rights.
- **Art. 54**: A witness may use written notes and documents when testifying. A witness must give truthful testimony and "**answer the questions of the judge and persons participating in the case**". The court warns the witness of criminal liability for knowingly false testimony and takes a signed acknowledgment (cross-reference to Criminal Code Arts. 238 and 240). No one is obliged to testify against themselves or close relatives.
- **Arts. 56, 58, 60**: Experts and specialists may question participants and witnesses only **with the court's permission**. An interpreter may ask questions to clarify a translation.
- **Art. 68**: Each party proves what it relies on. Evidence must be disclosed to the other participants before the hearing or within the court's deadline, and "participants may rely only on evidence the other participants were **acquainted with in advance**". If a party withholds evidence the court requested, its contents are **presumed to be against that party and deemed admitted**.
- **Art. 88**: A witness is called on a participant's motion, which must say which circumstances the witness can confirm. The witness reports orally, and in writing if the court proposes it. **If the witness cannot indicate the source of their information, it is not evidence.**
- **Art. 89**: Explanations of participants are examined and assessed together with other evidence. A participant's admission of facts is **not binding** on the court. The court may treat it as established if it matches the circumstances and was not made under deception, violence, threat or misleading, or to conceal the truth.
- **Art. 167**: Participants address the court as "**Hurmatli sud!**" and give explanations, testimony and answers standing. For a breach of order the presiding judge **warns** the offender. On a repeat breach, the offender may be **removed** from the courtroom.
- **Art. 168**: In the hearing the court examines evidence. It hears participants' explanations, witness testimony, expert opinions and specialist advice, and examines physical, written and **digital** evidence.
- **Art. 169**: Motions on all hearing issues are decided after hearing the other participants, by a ruling (ajrim).
- **Art. 172**: After a recess, evidence already examined is not re-examined, even if a representative is replaced.
- **Art. 173 (judicial debates, sud muzokaralari)**: The claimant speaks first, then third parties, then the respondent. Participants "**may not rely on circumstances not established by the court, or on evidence not examined at the hearing or ruled inadmissible**".
- **Art. 174**: The court may reopen examination of evidence during or after the debates.

### Inferences (how to translate the techniques)
- **"Objection!" becomes a motion or statement to the court.** The student says: "Hurmatli sud, I ask that the question be rephrased / not be put, because it (a) assumes a fact not established (Art. 173 by analogy, Art. 68), (b) misquotes the protocol, (c) combines two questions, (d) is insulting (Art. 167)." The court decides; Art. 169 contemplates a ruling. The student should ask for the statement to be **recorded in the minutes (bayonnoma)** to preserve it for appeal.
- **Leading questions are not prohibited by the EPC text**, so do not "object to leading". Instead, attack the *weight*: answers suggested by the question, and witness information without a source (Art. 88), are weak evidence. In Russian criminal procedure leading questions are banned by statute. Students coming from criminal courses may wrongly assume the same rule applies here. This is an inference; the Russian criminal-procedure provision was not fetched.
- **Questions to the other side's representative.** Art. 42 lets participants ask questions of each other. The representative is not a witness and is not under Art. 54 liability. Answers are "explanations" assessed under Art. 89. The counter for students: answer facts the client authorized, say "I will clarify with my principal" when unsure, and never make an unauthorized admission. An admission by a representative can also be challenged under Art. 89's misleading and concealment safeguards.
- **Ambush documents** are blocked by Art. 68 (only previously disclosed evidence). The AI opponent should lawfully disclose in advance and then use the documents for impeachment (T7, T8). An "ambush" mode can let students practice the Art. 68 objection.
- **Closing (sud muzokaralari).** Younger 10 ("save it for summation") fits Art. 173. It also limits the AI: it may argue only from established and examined evidence.
- **Timing.** Because evidence is not re-examined after a recess (Art. 172), a student who misses an objection may lose it. Train immediate reactions.
- **Ethics anchor for LexSim scoring:** the good-faith duty (Art. 42), the order rules (Art. 167), the misleading safeguard (Art. 89), and the limits on debate arguments (Art. 173). Together with the foreign authorities on insulting or intimidating questions, these mark the line. Misconduct includes misrepresenting evidence, inventing facts, misquoting, insulting or intimidating, and using forged documents.
